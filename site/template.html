Give the work its right shape.
Then prove it.

You have a job you want AI to help with. Before anyone builds anything, Right Shape answers three questions: what form should it take, how much should it do on its own, and what kind of AI, if any, does it need.

It explains each answer in plain words and gives you a test that shows whether the result works. Most of the time the right answer is smaller than you expected, and that is the point.

How the freedom is decided

What it wants.
What it can safely have.

Ambition sets the need. Safety sets the ceiling. The skill never lets the first overrule the second, and when they disagree it says what would honestly close the gap.

Autonomy is a design decision, separate from how capable the model is. A strong model may, and often should, run low on the ladder.

N

Need

How open the path is, how much context it carries, how long it runs without anyone watching, and whether it acts on its own. Four scores from 0 to 3 map to a rung.

C

Ceiling

Six gates: can the result be checked, can it be undone, is the lethal trifecta broken, is it isolated, is there a budget, is someone accountable. Each failure caps the rung.

Three modes, one rubric

Place. Audit.
Check.

Place one job and get a card. Audit a set of repositories and get a report with one disposition for every item. Check one capability against the contract and get its gaps, removals first.

The ceiling comes first.

No placement above the lowest failed gate.

When the job wants more than it can safely have, there are two honest moves: lower the need (decompose it, fix the path into a recipe, seal stable expertise in a Smart Tool) or raise the ceiling (add an evaluator, put irreversible calls behind approval, break a trifecta leg, isolate it, set budgets, name an owner).
